# Enterprise-D Bridge Fidelity Research

Date: 2026-10-20
Question: What documented dimensions, images, and 3D models exist for the Star Trek: The Next Generation main bridge, can any model be used under a permissive license, and otherwise how should Agent Helm rebuild the bridge from primary references?

## Short answer

No freely licensed, game-ready Enterprise-D bridge model was found that could be dropped into Agent Helm. The best path is a rebuild driven by three primary sources: Rick Sternbach's official blueprint set for the plan and scale, the studio construction blueprint dimensions quoted by a holder of that set (28.75 ft wide, 40 ft long for the set), and modern photographic reference from the Picard season 3 rebuild and the Roddenberry Archive 1:1 bridge tours. Our current bridge is about 15.2 m wide against a set width of 8.76 m, which is the main reason it feels empty.

## Documented dimensions

### Studio construction blueprints (strongest set evidence)

A holder of the original studio construction blueprints for the 1701-D bridge posted the two headline dimensions on The RPF forum thread "TREK Enterprise D scaled replica bridge?": width 28.75 ft across the back edge of the computer walls, length 40 ft from the viewscreen to the edge of the rear console. In metric that is 8.76 m by 12.19 m. The same thread mentions a recollection that Roddenberry insisted on a dimension of 31 ft; that figure is second-hand and unverified, so treat the 28.75 by 40 ft pair as the anchor and 31 ft as a rumour to check against the blueprint plan.

Source: https://www.therpf.com/forums/threads/trek-enterprise-d-scaled-replica-bridge.113024/

TrekBBS runs a long "Set blueprint exchange" thread where members trade scans of production set blueprints, including TNG standing sets. It is the most likely place to obtain the full bridge construction set short of buying it.

Source: https://www.trekbbs.com/threads/set-blueprint-exchange.244483/

### Sternbach blueprints (best published plan)

Star Trek: The Next Generation U.S.S. Enterprise NCC-1701-D Blueprints, Rick Sternbach, 1996, thirteen sheets at 22 by 34 inches. Screen 4 carries plan views of decks 1 and 2, which is the bridge, ready room, and observation lounge at a stated scale with a scale bar. This is the sheet to digitise and measure station by station. The set is out of print but common second-hand.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek:_The_Next_Generation_U.S.S._Enterprise_NCC-1701-D_Blueprints and https://memory-beta.fandom.com/wiki/USS_Enterprise_NCC-1701-D_Blueprints

### Technical Manual and other print

The Star Trek: The Next Generation Technical Manual (Sternbach and Okuda, 1991) has the bridge chapter with the plan diagram and station descriptions. Good for layout intent, weaker than the blueprint sheet for measurements. The Eaglemoss Official Starships Collection Enterprise-D special has bridge under-construction photography and design history, useful for wall and ceiling construction detail.

Sources: https://wixiban.com/downloads/em-mags-special/special-mag-20-gold-uss-enterprise-ncc-1701-d.pdf

### Measured props and consoles

Star Trek Prop Authority published a measurement study of a Star Trek: The Experience operations console replica built to production patterns: about 32 inches high with a 41.5 inch wide monitor unit on top (0.81 m high, 1.05 m wide). A screen-used Engineering LCARS panel measured 51 by 13.5 inches (1.30 by 0.34 m). These give console and panel proportions that our current consoles overshoot.

Sources: http://www.startrekpropauthority.com/2012/09/star-trek-experience-tng-operations.html and http://www.startrekpropauthority.com/2015/03/star-trek-tng-engineering-lcars-panel.html

## Images and visual reference

- Picard season 3 (2023): the bridge was rebuilt from scratch from the original blueprints with Mike and Denise Okuda, using period tools and the same type of wood. Production designers Dave Blass and Liz Kloczkowski. This is the best modern photographic reference, shot in HD from many angles. Sources: https://www.cbr.com/picard-season-3-tng-enterprise-rebuilt-perfect/ and https://screenrant.com/star-trek-picard-tng-enterprise-d-build-explained/
- Roddenberry Archive with OTOY: free 1:1 scale virtual tours of every Enterprise bridge in a browser, built for historical accuracy. Best interactive reference for proportions and sightlines; view-only, nothing to download. Source: https://whatculture.com/film/how-otoy-just-changed-star-trek-forever
- Stage 9 (fan project, 2016 to 2018): a full Enterprise-D in Unreal Engine with a highly detailed bridge, playable in VR. CBS issued a cease and desist in September 2018 and the build is not legally distributed. Walkthrough videos survive and are excellent motion reference. Sources: https://kotaku.com/cbs-shuts-down-ambitious-fan-effort-to-make-a-virtual-s-1829396289 and https://trektoday.com/content/2018/09/stage-9-fan-enterprise-d-recreation-shut-down/
- Star Trek: Bridge Crew, The Next Generation DLC (Ubisoft Red Storm, 2018): the Enterprise-D bridge recreated with CBS assistance from blueprints and digital assets, for VR. Commercial and not extractable for reuse, but a strong proportion reference. Source: https://www.githyp.com/star-trek-bridge-crew-134923/?tab=news
- On Board the U.S.S. Enterprise (Okuda and Okuda, 2013): book plus CD-ROM 3D tour of bridge, engineering, and sickbay. Source: https://blog.trekcore.com/2013/02/new-preview-images-from-on-board-the-uss-enterprise/
- TrekCore episode galleries: thousands of HD screencaps for LCARS colours, carpet tone, and station details per season. Source: https://tng.trekcore.com/

## 3D models found

- Free3D "Enterprise D Bridge", 149 USD, 3ds Max and V-Ray, 8 MB. Paid, licence for the buyer only, not permissive, Max format needs conversion. https://free3d.com/3d-model/enterprise-d-bridge-5228.html
- 3DExport "starship - startrek bridge", about 18 USD, GLB at about 105 MB. Paid and far too heavy for Quest as shipped; could serve as paid reference only. https://3dexport.com/3dmodel-starship-startrek-bridge-354243.htm
- CGTrader "Star Trek 25th century Bridge". Paid, royalty-free licence for the buyer, not the TNG bridge exactly. https://www.cgtrader.com/3d-models/space/spaceship/star-trek-25th-century-bridge
- Thingiverse "TNG Deck 1 - Bridge" by Mike1701, free. A print-scale deck 1 model. Thingiverse listings carry Creative Commons licences chosen per item; this item's exact licence was not confirmed in this research and must be checked on the page before any use. Geometry at print scale is a useful plan cross-check, not a game asset. Indexed at https://www.stlfinder.com/3dmodels/Enterprise+D+Bridge/
- Spaceways 1/650 Enterprise-D bridge and conference room interior, paid print files via Thangs. Print detail only. https://thangs.com/prints/designer/Spaceways/3d-model/1%2F650%20Enterprise%20D%20Bridge%2FConference%20Room-1352760
- Star Trek Voyager: Elite Force fan map "USS Enterprise-D Bridge", free download as a game level. Fan licence, BSP format, dated graphics. Reference at best. https://www.lonebullet.com/maps/download-uss-enterprise-d-bridge-demo-star-trek-voyager-elite-force-map-level-free-13090.htm
- Sketchfab hosts several downloadable fan Enterprise bridges, mostly TOS or Enterprise-E, under per-model Creative Commons licences. No TNG main bridge of usable quality surfaced in search. Start at https://sketchfab.com/tags/startrek-starships

Licence note for Agent Helm as a public project at agenthelm.dev with a public GitHub repo: only a model under CC0, CC BY, or CC BY-SA with the licence verified on its page could be shipped in the repo, and even CC BY requires credit in the app. Star Trek itself is CBS and Paramount intellectual property regardless of any model licence; that is a separate risk the project already carries by using the bridge at all, and it argues for keeping the repo clearly non-commercial fan work.

## What this means for our bridge

Measured against the set dimensions, our current room is 15.2 m across the wall diameter against 8.76 m across the back computer walls, and 12.8 m long against 12.19 m. The length is close; the width is about 70 percent too wide, which is why the floor reads as empty and the consoles read as small. Ceiling height was not found as a published number; from set photography it is roughly 3.4 to 3.8 m, close to our 3.65 m. Our ops console top is about right in spirit but our station screens are larger than the measured 41.5 inch (1.05 m) monitor unit.

## Recommendation

Rebuild from images and written dimensions, in this order:

1. Get the plan: obtain the Sternbach blueprint Screen 4 (buy the set or find the scan in the TrekBBS exchange), scale it from its scale bar, and extract a station table in metres: room outline, viewscreen width and height, conn and ops position and angle, command chair trio, tactical horseshoe radius, aft station positions, turbolift doors, ramp runs.
2. Reset the room shell to the studio set box first: 8.76 m across the aft computer walls, 12.19 m viewscreen to aft console, oval fitted inside that, ceiling about 3.6 m. Keep the in-universe station names and our screen system.
3. Rebuild furniture from the measured props: consoles about 0.81 m high with about 1.05 m wide display units, LCARS panels in the measured ratios, captain chair scaled from set photography.
4. Dress from Picard season 3 and Roddenberry Archive reference: carpet tone, wood rail, wall panel rhythm, ceiling dome, ready room door and observation lounge doors as set dressing.
5. Keep a paid model as an optional measuring reference only if the blueprint route stalls. The Free3D Max model is the closest candidate; do not ship its geometry in the public repo without legal advice.

## Unresolved

- Exact ceiling height, viewscreen width and height, and horseshoe radius need the blueprint sheet or a good scan; they are not reliably published as numbers on the open web.
- The 31 ft recollection on The RPF is unverified and may refer to an earlier design stage.
- The Thingiverse item licence and the Elite Force map licence need a page-level check before any use beyond looking.
